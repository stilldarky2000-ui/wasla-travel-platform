# Professional flags, stated plainly

**The sea trip price ladder is very tight, and Orange Bay is now the problem child.** At €20, Orange Bay, the most famous island name in Hurghada, is priced barely above Dolphin House (€17) and below Hula Hula (€22). Guests who know the market will read €20 one of two ways: a bargain, or a boat they do not want to be on. Established operators sell this day at €30 to €45. If the €20 is real and sustainable, lean into it hard in the gallery and the first two lines of copy, because price is now this trip's main conversion weapon. If it is a promotional price, say so on the page before guests anchor on it permanently.

**Costs are in Egyptian pounds, prices are in euros.** Every one of these EUR prices was built from an EGP supplier cost plus 20 percent. A sharp move in the exchange rate quietly eats the margin, and at €17 and €20 there is not much margin to eat. Put a date in the diary to re-check these prices against the rate, or one bad month turns the two cheapest trips into loss leaders you never meant to offer.

**Dolphin House at €17 will sell, and that is the risk.** A full day with transfers, gear, buffet and drinks at €17 sets an expectation of a budget boat, and budget boats collect budget reviews: crowded deck, rushed stops, average food. The copy is honest about the dolphins, which protects you there. Nothing protects you if the actual boat experience feels cheap. This trip will likely become your volume product; its reviews will define the brand more than the premium trip will.

**Paradise Island Premium lives or dies on two promises: fewer guests and lunch on the island.** Both are in the copy because both were supplied as facts. If a busy week tempts anyone to fill that boat, do not do it quietly: a guest who paid €37 for a short guest list and finds a full boat writes the worst review in your catalogue, and they will be right. Same with the lunch. The day it is served on board instead, the premium story collapses.

**The speed boat needs a capacity number and a time boundary before it goes on sale.** €101 fixed for the whole boat is a clean, attractive offer, and the first thing every group will ask is "how many people fit" and "how long do we get". Right now the facts answer neither. Without a stated capacity and an agreed duration window, expect negotiation at the marina, disappointment about trip length, and reviews that say the trip felt short. Get both numbers from the operator and put them in the copy.

**The quad trip's "no meals" needs to survive contact with the booking page, not just this copy.** It is stated three times in the trip text, including in the sunset option, because the single most predictable complaint on a 3-hour sunset quad trip is a hungry guest who assumed dinner. Make sure the checkout summary and the confirmation message repeat it. The same applies in reverse to the Super Safari: its program and €20 price were confirmed by the operator on 2026-11-24 and its copy above is now complete, so the two safari trips can sit next to each other in the catalogue, one clearly without meals and one clearly with dinner and a show.

**Star Gazing is priced at €22 with a jeep, transfers and dinner included. That is very thin.** One couple's no-show can erase a departure's profit, and the competing listings promise telescopes, Bedouin camps and shows that this copy honestly cannot promise yet, because the facts were not supplied. Either get the full program and price the trip on what it actually is, or accept that this trip is a reputation product, not a money product. Also note the copy currently cannot say when guests get back, and guests with children will ask.

**Zero reviews means the photos carry everything.** Every claim in this copy (white sand, quiet island, colourful reef, dark sky) will be judged against the gallery in about four seconds. The trips whose galleries are thin or borrowed-looking will not convert at any price, and the premium trip in particular needs real photos of an uncrowded boat and lunch on the island, or its own copy becomes evidence against it.

**"Drinks all day" is doing quiet damage.** It appears on every sea trip because it was supplied that way, but it never says what the drinks are. European guests will read it as soft drinks and water, mostly correctly, until one guest expects more and writes about it. One word fixes it everywhere: name the drinks (water, soft drinks, tea and coffee, whatever is true) in the included list of each sea trip. I left the wording as supplied rather than invent specifics, but this should be corrected with the real list before launch.

**Pickup logistics will generate more complaints than the trips will.** Five of these products leave between 08:30 and 09:00 and collect from a zone that stretches from El Gouna to Safaga. Guests in the far zones will be picked up very early and will spend a long time on the bus collecting others. The copy promises a confirmed pickup time the day before, which is good, but set expectations in the confirmation message about the collection route, or the first reviews will be about the bus, not the boat.
